# Upside Arena agent notes

- **No em dashes, and nothing else that reads as generated, in anything a person sees. The whole of `src/`, not a list of screens.** Product copy has to sound like a person wrote it, because in Arena every sentence is one: there is no model-written copy in this app at all, so nothing was scrubbing them and nothing was going to. A dash used as a clause break is the loudest single tell that a sentence was generated, so reader-facing strings take a comma, a colon or a full stop instead, and number ranges are "2 to 3 weeks" or "2028-2029". `tests/unit/reader-copy.test.ts` walks **every** `.ts`/`.tsx` under `src/` and fails on an em or en dash in any reader-facing line. It skips block comments, `//` comments (string-aware, so a URL is not mistaken for one) and `*.test.ts*`, because source is exempt: the rule is about the product. `ALLOWED` is an **exception** list, currently one file (`src/lib/brand/mono-subset.ts`, which names the characters in a Unicode block so the subsetter keeps them in the font). A test keeps it honest: an entry naming a file that no longer contains a dash fails, so it cannot become a parking space for unfixed copy. **Never add a file to `ALLOWED` to make a failure go away.** A separate scan for AI-tell vocabulary (delve, seamless, robust, elevate, empower, cutting-edge, in today's fast-paced, and forty more) found **nothing** in hand-written copy: the dashes were the whole problem. `/how` carried twelve of them and is now clean.
- **A cell with no number in it says `NO_VALUE`, and `NO_VALUE` is "n/a".** `src/lib/format.ts` exports it; it is the fallback in every `Score` card on Home, in a battle and in a record, and in `StandingsTable`, `SeasonTable` and `Lineup`. It was an em dash. **Do not "fix" it to a bare hyphen**: these sit beside signed percentages in `tabular-nums` columns where a loss already renders with a leading `-`, so a lone `-` reads as a number whose digits failed to load.

- **The signed-out page is a product page, and it is the same page Lab's is.** `SignedOutLanding` (`src/components/`) is composed by `src/app/page.tsx` and follows Upside Lab's flow deliberately, because the two apps are one design and this is the first screen anybody sees of it: problem, the interface directly under it, what it does, how you start, the rest of the rooms, what it costs beside what is at stake, then the ask again, then a footer. What it used to be was a sign-in box with the game in two lines beside it, which meant a stranger judged Arena on two of the eight things it does with no answer to what it costs or what is at stake. Every claim on it lives in `src/lib/product.ts` so the page, the metadata and `/how` cannot end up describing three slightly different games, and nothing aspirational goes in that file. The page stays a **server component** and keeps its Suspense boundaries around the sign-in button alone, so `/` is still Partial Prerender; `Arrive` is the one client piece and it is a wrapper.
- **Three rules hold this page that hold nothing else in the app, and they are all restraint.** (1) **One headline, one line under it, then the thing.** No eyebrow above every section in small coloured capitals: that pattern is everywhere in software marketing and nowhere in Arena, so a page wearing it reads as a page *about* Arena rather than as Arena. Stills carry the app's own small muted label, not invented chrome, because they are there to be believed. (2) **A card gets one line.** The first draft ran three sentences under every heading and two more inside every card, and that much explaining does not read as thorough, it reads as doubt; anything needing a paragraph belongs on `/how`, which is linked and needs no account. (3) **Size is the argument**: 64px hero, 44px section headings, 16px body rather than 14px. The copy is short enough to afford it, and that is the trade. **Cut the sentence, not the type**: the hero's two lines are 26 and 19 characters because that is what holds one line each at 64px, and a 44-character draft forced 44px and *still* wrapped, at which point `text-wrap: balance` split it mid-phrase.
- **One width, and every pane on the page shares its edges.** `max-w-5xl`, hero card included. The stills briefly sat in a 1200px container on the theory that a picture of the app should be as wide as the app, which put three different left edges down one page (336, 120, 208 at 1440px). That does not read as emphasis, it reads as a page assembled from parts: the eye tracks a vertical edge far more readily than it infers a container's intent. The only narrower measure is the run of centred text in the hero, which is a measure rather than an edge. Section heads are centred for the same reason, since the hero and the closing ask both are.
- **Google is the only way in, and that is why the closing ask is a real button.** The magic link went on 2026-08-23 (`signInWithEmail`, `email-mx.ts` and the address-suggestion flow with it). Everything it did served getting an address right that Google already has right, and every step of it was a way for somebody to fail to reach their own account. Because signing in is now one button, the ask at the bottom is the ask rather than a link back up to one, which is what Lab's does; while the email field existed it had to be an anchor, since a second copy meant a second form, a second control labelled "Email" and one visitor counted twice. **`signin_viewed` is on the page, not in the card** (`TrackView` in `page.tsx`), because the card renders twice. **The consent sentence renders beside *every* sign-in button**, not once at the top: consent belongs where the action is. The advice disclaimer and the support address moved out of the hero into the footer, because four lines of grey small print under the only control on the screen is the loudest possible way to say there is small print. `terms_acceptances`, written at onboarding, is still the durable record of who agreed to what, so nothing about that record depended on the magic link. The signed-out suite needs `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`/`GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` placeholders the way it already needed Supabase ones, set in `playwright.config.ts` and in the CI job; without them the button does not render and half the landing tests fail for a reason unrelated to the change under review.
- **`Arrive` never hides anything already on screen.** `src/components/Arrive.tsx` measures the rect first and marks a section arrived without passing through the hidden state when it is at or above the fold. Without that check, hydrating hides what the reader is looking at and fades it back a frame later, and anybody who arrived part way down the page, from a link with a hash, a reload, or restored scroll position, watches the page they are reading disappear. The attribute is written straight onto the node rather than through React state, and a browser with no `IntersectionObserver` gets no attribute at all and a page that is simply visible. **Do not put `scroll-behavior: smooth` back on the landing.** It was there for the `#start` anchor, the anchor is gone with the email field, and it made every programmatic scroll-into-view animate, which is how a click probe in `dock.spec.ts` started landing before the scroll settled.
- **Nothing on the landing arrives where a reader can watch it.** `ARRIVE_LEAD` (`src/components/Arrive.tsx`) is **1.25**, a screen and a quarter, and it reaches the observer as a **positive** bottom `rootMargin`. It shipped as `-12%`, which is the opposite: a negative margin shrinks the observer's root rather than growing it, so measured on the real page every section flipped to "in" while sitting **116px to 185px inside the window** and only then started its fade. What a reader saw where the next section should be was an empty band and then something slowly appearing, which reads as the page having ended, so back up they went. The mount check is the same rule, not a second one: a section **inside the lead** at load is marked arrived without ever being hidden, so nothing flashes on the first screen, a reload part way down draws its surroundings, and the screenful after the one you are looking at is finished before you reach it. It used to stop at the fold, which left the section immediately below it fading in because somebody scrolled. **A heading and the row it heads go in one `Arrive`**: they were two, the cards on an 80ms delay, which is how a title came to sit above a hole, and there is no per-section delay any more. `Section` is `py-10 sm:py-12`, so 80px between sections on a phone and 96px on a desktop. It was `py-14 sm:py-20`, and the ratio that defends is the one against the section; the ratio a scrolling reader lives is against the **window**, where 160px is 18% of a 900px screen and was the tallest empty band on the page. The fade stays 0.55s and 12px, deliberately: with the lead above it nobody watches it, and the number was chosen with a reason beside it. Upside Lab's `Reveal` is this component with the same lead and the same guard, and it had the same bug for the same reason, so fix both or neither. `tests/unit/landing-scroll.test.ts` reads the source, and `tests/e2e/signed-out.spec.ts` walks the page in 100px steps and fails if any section is still unpainted while on screen.
- **The landing's light travels with the page. `.landing-field`, never `.page-frame` alone.** `.page-frame::before` is `position: fixed`, which is right in a room (the lobes are the room's lamps and a room is one viewport of content) and wrong on a page five screens tall, where the cyan corner you arrive on is the same cyan corner five screens later: what reads is not a lit room but a colour that stopped. `.landing-field` (`globals.css`) kills the fixed pair with `display: none` and puts five lobes on `::after`, sized in `vh` and positioned in `%` of the whole document, alternating `--primary` and `--glow-secondary` so scrolling passes through them. The magenta stays much quieter than the cyan, as it is everywhere else. Measured against `/how`: the fixed field reads 7,87,95 at the top of the page and 7,87,95 at the bottom; this one runs 6,68,75 / 26,15,26 / 4,48,53 / 25,14,25 / 4,51,56, and **the middle of the frame never rises above 4 out of 255**. That last number is the one that matters. Do not brighten it, and judge any change on the page middle rather than on how much of the field is lit.
- **The walkthrough's rows are `.glass`, not `CARD`.** `CARD` (`src/lib/page-shell.ts`) is `glass-well`, the quiet nested material keyed to `--muted`, and it is right for a well sitting inside a panel that is already doing the refraction. The walkthrough has no panel around it: its rows sit straight on a dialog over an 80% scrim, where `glass-well` reads as a flat grey box. `ROW_GLASS` in `src/components/WelcomeTour.tsx` uses the top-level material instead (`card-sheen glass`), which is what carries the three specular terms that sell glass on a near-black field. Anything else drawn directly on an overlay wants the same treatment.
- **One walkthrough, versioned, and everybody sees it once.** `WelcomeTour` (`src/components/`) opens over Home with Home already painted behind it. Whether it shows is `profiles.tour_version` against `TOUR_VERSION` (`src/lib/tour.ts`); raising the constant re-shows it to every account exactly once, and that is the whole reset mechanism. `needsTour` returns **false** for `undefined`/`null`, so a deploy that lands before its migration shows nobody a walkthrough rather than showing everybody one on every page load. Escape, the X and "Skip the tour" all write the version down: a walkthrough that returns tomorrow because you dismissed it today is a nag with a progress bar. Nothing in `src/lib/tour-steps.ts` is a figure typed by hand, and `tests/unit/tour.test.ts` holds it to that. Every screen is in `/gallery` so `tests/e2e/clipping.spec.ts` measures it at every width a phone reports.
- **The dock is chrome, not a card, and it is two shapes.** `BottomDock` is `card-sheen glass glass-dock`; `glass-dock` (`globals.css`) replaces the body and the blur only, `--background` at 55% and a 20px radius in place of the 2% white veil and 6px, leaving the rim, the ring and the lift shadow untouched. A card is built to let the room through; a dock sits over the bottom corner with content scrolling under it, and at the card's numbers it measures nearly **twice the colour of the field beside it**. Two constraints fix the 55% and it is the smallest number meeting both: the pane's own chroma must not exceed the field's, and muted label text must clear AAA over every pixel of the bar. Do not lower it to let more glow through, and do not raise it either. **Below `md` the dock is the phone's** (full width inside the page gutter, equal cells, glyph over label) **and at `md` and up it is the desktop's** (content-hugging, centred, glyph beside label). Upside Lab's `MobileTabBar` and `BookModeDock` are the same pair, and the two apps are meant to stay one design. **Never hide a dock label at a breakpoint.** `max-[544px]:sr-only` did exactly that, which is to say on every phone anybody owns, so five rooms arrived as five unlabelled glyphs: a trophy is not a word, and nothing about it says Leagues rather than Season. The stacked cell is what makes a label affordable at 320px. `tests/e2e/dock.spec.ts` asserts no label is hidden and measures every label against **the padding box of its own cell**, because a row that fits the screen tells you nothing about a word inside a 52px cell. Concentric corners: `rounded-xl` shell, `p-1`, `rounded-lg` cells (12 - 4 = 8). Full account, with the tables, in `globals.css` beside `.glass-dock`.
- **Glass is a 2% white veil with all of its light on the rim, and the black inside a pane is the black of the room.** The body is `--glass-veil` (`oklch(1 0 0 / 2%)`), **white and achromatic, never a tinted card**. Two measured reasons. (1) A tinted fill leaves a hue behind: thinned over a lit field a neutral card lands somewhere between its own bias and the room's, which is a colour nobody chose. White has no hue to leave. (2) **The veil's alpha is what sets the black floor** — the old fill lifted a pane about nine luminance levels out of 255 above the field beside it, and a pane whose blacks are grey is a card, not glass; at 2% the lift is near zero. **`--glass-contrast` (1.05) is the second lever and is not optional**: contrast pivots on mid-grey so it leaves true black alone, but this field is not true black (the cyan and magenta lobes sit at about five levels) and without it a pane lifts those six. Measured 1.00 → +6.1, 1.02 → +3.1, 1.05 → +0.1. **Saturation is free**: 1.75 and 2.40 measure identically on black lift. `--glass-blur` is **6px, not 40** — past roughly 7px a blur stops refracting and starts mixing adjacent hues into mud. The rim came down with the fill (`--glass-rim-top` 38%, not 56%) because at this transparency the old weight read as a drawn outline rather than as light; it still takes the room's two hues, cyan on top from `--primary` and magenta underneath from `--glow-secondary`, with the sides unequal because the key light is off the left. **Wells are panes in their own right** (`--glass-veil-well` 3%, own 5px blur, softest rim in the system), not grey boxes on a pane. `.glass-notice`, `.glass-bar` and `.glass-dock` keep heavier fills and a real blur because they sit over content, which is the only place a heavy blur earns its keep. `.glass-dock`'s fill is keyed to `--background` rather than `--card`, so its alpha is exactly how much of the ambient glow the dock eats and it cannot leave a hue behind. Never reintroduce a falloff, a `background-image` ramp, or a tinted card fill on any glass surface. Judge a change by measuring black lift and the material's own chroma against `/gallery`, never by eyeballing.
- **One account, however many mailboxes, and the player tag never moves.** A person signs up on a laptop with a work address and then taps Continue with Google on their phone, which is a different address and, until now, a different account: a second player tag, a record starting at zero, and a league nobody is in. `account_emails` (migration `0025`) is the list of other addresses that open one account. **No second auth user is ever made.** Supabase still holds exactly one, with one primary email, and the extra addresses are checked before a sign-in is turned into a session: `signInWithEmail` and the Google callback both ask `accountForAddress` first, and when it answers, the session comes from a one-time token minted for the account that owns the address (`magicTokenFor`, spent by `verifyOtp`) rather than from Supabase's own idea of who that address is. Two ways to add one, because two kinds of mailbox arrive differently. **Google proves itself in the handshake and is added on the spot**: the same custom flow, with `link` written into the state cookie before the browser leaves, because nothing coming back from Google can say what the trip was for. **Anything else is mailed a link** carrying a token whose sha256 is all the table holds, good for one hour, and `/auth/link` confirms the address and deliberately **signs nobody in**: a link in an unconfirmed mailbox that opened somebody's account would be the thing this feature exists to prevent. Adding is the service role's work (the check is the whole job, and a client checking itself is not a check); the one write a client may make to that table is deleting a row of its own, and row level security says so. **The one destructive case is narrow and the database decides it.** An address that already has an Arena account can be taken only when `account_never_played` says that account has no tag, no finished onboarding, no trade, no league, no shared week and nothing bought, which is somebody who signed in once, was asked for a player tag and closed the tab. Two accounts that have both been played are refused and sent to support, because choosing which one loses its weeks is not a decision code gets to make. Every outcome is a word in `ADDRESS_MESSAGES` (`src/lib/auth/address-link.ts`) and the sentence lives beside it, because the Google leg comes back as a redirect and can only carry the word. Do not replace any of this with Supabase's `linkIdentity`: it sends the browser to Supabase's own callback, which is the exact thing `src/lib/auth/google.ts` refuses to do, and it would put a hostname nobody recognises on the consent screen.
